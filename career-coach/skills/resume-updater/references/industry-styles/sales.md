# Sales — Industry Style Reference

Formatting instructions for resumes, cover letters, and career documents targeting sales,
business development, and account management roles: SDR, BDR, AE, Enterprise AE, Account
Manager, Sales Manager, Sales Director. Read this file when `preferences.industry_style` in
career-profile.json is `sales`. Follow these rules exactly.

## Section Order

Use this order, top to bottom:

1. **Header** — name, email, phone, location, LinkedIn
2. **Experience (revenue-first)** — first and dominant; sales hiring managers read for one
   thing: did this person consistently hit number, and how big was the number
3. **Key Achievements** — a short highlight band (3-4 lines) surfacing career-best numbers:
   President's Club years, biggest deal, best attainment year; this survives the 6-second scan
   even if the reader never reaches page bottom
4. **Skills** — methodology, CRM, verticals, sales tech (see format below)
5. **Education** — last and brief; in sales, track record outranks credentials

Rationale: a sales resume is itself a sales document. Lead with proof of revenue, make the
career-best numbers impossible to miss, and keep everything else supporting.

## The Iron Rule

**Every bullet needs a number.** In sales, a bullet without one reads as a missed quota. If
the candidate can't quantify an accomplishment, ask for the number (deal size? attainment?
rank on team?) or cut the bullet. Never estimate a figure for them: sales numbers get checked
in interviews and reference calls. Acceptable numbers: revenue, %, quota attainment, deal count, rank, cycle length,
win rate, pipeline generated, accounts, territory size, retention/expansion rates.

## Bullet Writing Rules

- Metric families, in order of persuasive power:
  - **Revenue closed**: $ closed-won per year, ARR/MRR added, TCV of largest deals
  - **Quota attainment**: % of quota by year ("117% of $1.2M quota, FY24") — always pair the
    percentage with the quota size; 150% of a tiny number impresses no one
  - **Deal mechanics**: average deal size, sales cycle length, win rate, pipeline coverage
  - **Scope**: accounts managed, territory size/geography, segment (SMB/MM/Enterprise)
  - **Rank**: "#2 of 24 AEs", President's Club, Rookie of the Year
- Structure: verb → number → context → how ("Closed $2.1M in new ARR (124% of quota) by
  building a champion-led outbound motion into the healthcare vertical").
- Show year-over-year progression where it exists — a rising attainment trend is the
  strongest signal on the page.
- 3-5 bullets per role; first bullet is always that role's best revenue or attainment number.

## Example Bullets

Use these as the voice and density target — every bullet has a number:

1. Closed $3.4M in new ARR in FY24 (131% of $2.6M quota), ranking #1 of 18 enterprise AEs
   and earning President's Club for the second consecutive year.
2. Landed the company's largest-ever deal ($1.1M TCV, 3-year term) by running a 9-month
   MEDDIC-qualified cycle across 14 stakeholders including the CFO and CISO.
3. Grew territory revenue from $1.8M to $4.2M in two years by rebuilding the account plan
   around 12 named enterprise logos and a partner co-sell motion.
4. Generated $6.8M in qualified pipeline (4.1x coverage) through 60 outbound touches/day and
   a personalized video sequence that lifted reply rates from 3% to 11%.
5. Shortened average sales cycle from 94 to 61 days by introducing mutual close plans and a
   security-review fast-track, lifting quarterly win rate from 22% to 31%.
6. Expanded 28 existing accounts by $1.9M in upsell/cross-sell ARR (net revenue retention
   134%) through quarterly business reviews tied to customer ROI dashboards.
7. Promoted from SDR to AE in 11 months after booking 212 qualified meetings (146% of target)
   and sourcing $2.3M in pipeline, of which $840K closed-won.
8. Managed a $5.5M renewal book across 40 mid-market accounts with 96% gross retention,
   saving 6 at-risk accounts worth $700K through executive escalation playbooks.
9. Hired, ramped, and led a 6-rep SDR team to 118% of team quota; 4 of 6 reps promoted to AE
   within 18 months.
10. Won 9 competitive displacements worth $2.2M against the category leader using a
    Challenger-style insight deck and side-by-side TCO analysis.
11. Rebuilt Salesforce pipeline hygiene (stage definitions, exit criteria, weekly scrubs),
    improving forecast accuracy from ±30% to ±8% and cutting slipped deals by 40%.

## Skills Format

- **Sales Methodology**: MEDDIC/MEDDPICC, Challenger, SPIN, Sandler, Command of the Message
- **CRM & Pipeline**: Salesforce, HubSpot, Clari, Gong
- **Sales Tech**: Outreach, Salesloft, ZoomInfo, LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo
- **Verticals**: the industries actually sold into (SaaS, healthcare, fintech, manufacturing)
- **Motion**: outbound/inbound, PLG-assist, channel/partner, land-and-expand

## Tone and Formatting Rules

- Energetic and results-driven, but numbers do the bragging — no "dynamic self-starter" filler.
- Strong verbs: closed, won, landed, expanded, displaced, generated, negotiated, retained.
- Dollar figures use $ and abbreviations ($1.2M, $480K); percentages always as digits.
- Strictly one page below director level.
- Never hide a bad year by omitting dates; address gaps in the cover letter or interview.

## Career Ladder Framing

Frame advice against the standard progression: SDR → AE → Senior AE → Enterprise AE →
Sales Manager → Director → VP Sales, with the IC-vs-management fork at senior AE. Growth
signals: attainment consistency (3+ years over 100%), President's Club, segment moves
(SMB → MM → Enterprise), territory or vertical expansion, and deal-size progression. For
management-track candidates, emphasize team quota, hiring, and rep development numbers.

## Interview Weighting

Allocate prep time roughly:

- **Role-play / mock sales call: 40%** — live discovery or pitch of the company's own
  product; practice objection handling and closing for next steps
- **Pipeline review / deal walk-through: 30%** — present past deals like a forecast call:
  numbers, stages, stakeholders, why won/lost; interviewers probe for process rigor
- **Behavioral with revenue stories: 20%** — STAR stories where the Result is always a number
- **Industry and product knowledge: 10%** — the company's market, competitors, buyer personas

Cover letters for sales roles: lead with the single best revenue number in the first two
sentences, mirror the company's segment and motion, and close with a confident, specific ask
for the conversation — the letter itself should demonstrate how the candidate sells.
