# Consulting — Industry Style Reference

Formatting instructions for resumes, cover letters, and career documents targeting management
consulting, strategy, and advisory roles: MBB, Big 4, boutique firms, and internal strategy
teams. Read this file when `preferences.industry_style` in career-profile.json is `consulting`.
Follow these rules exactly.

## Section Order

Use this order, top to bottom:

1. **Header** — name, email, phone, location, LinkedIn
2. **Experience (client impact first)** — engagements framed as problem → approach →
   quantified client outcome; this is the evidence of structured problem-solving firms buy
3. **Education** — second, and deliberately more prominent than in other industries: consulting
   firms weight pedigree, GPA, honors, and test scores well into a candidate's career. Include
   GPA (if 3.5+), honors, scholarships, and relevant coursework or MBA concentration.
4. **Skills / Frameworks** — analytical toolkit, industries served, functional expertise
5. **Leadership & Activities** — last but never omitted; firms screen for "spike" evidence:
   leadership in organizations, competitions, published thought pieces

Rationale: consulting hiring evaluates structured thinking, client credibility, and polish.
Client impact proves the first two; education and leadership round out the firm's sell of the
candidate to its own clients.

## Bullet Writing Rules

- Every bullet needs a quantified outcome. Metric families:
  - **Client value**: $ value created (revenue, savings, EBITDA lift), % improvement
  - **Scope**: engagement size, client size (Fortune 500, $2B retailer), geography, workstreams
  - **Team**: people led or coached, cross-functional stakeholders, client seniority engaged
  - **Strategic outcomes**: markets entered, deals closed/diligenced, transformations launched
- Structure top-down (answer first): outcome, then approach — mirror the pyramid principle
  the firm itself uses ("Identified $12M in procurement savings by ... " not "Analyzed spend
  data which eventually led to ...").
- Anonymize clients by descriptor ("a $3B consumer goods company"), never by name unless public.
- Name the analytical method when it carries weight: market sizing, due diligence, MECE issue
  trees, hypothesis-driven analysis, operating model design.
- 3-5 bullets per engagement-heavy role; group short engagements under one employer heading.

## Example Bullets

Use these as the voice and density target — every bullet has a metric:

1. Identified $12M in annual procurement savings (8% of addressable spend) for a $2B
   manufacturer by leading a 6-week category analysis across 4 workstreams and negotiating
   pilot terms with 3 strategic suppliers.
2. Built the market-entry strategy for a European fintech expanding to LATAM: sized a $1.8B
   addressable market, prioritized 2 of 6 countries via a weighted opportunity matrix, and
   secured board approval for a $40M investment.
3. Led commercial due diligence on a $350M healthcare-services acquisition in 3 weeks —
   30 expert interviews and a 5-year revenue model — supporting a bid revision that saved the
   client $28M.
4. Designed the post-merger integration plan for two retail banks (9,000 combined FTEs),
   sequencing 120 initiatives into a 24-month roadmap that delivered $65M of a targeted $80M
   in synergies by month 18.
5. Reduced order-to-delivery time 35% for a $1.5B distributor by re-mapping the fulfillment
   operating model and piloting the redesign in 2 regions before national rollout.
6. Managed a 4-consultant workstream and 6 client analysts through a pricing transformation
   that lifted gross margin 240 bps ($31M annualized) across 11,000 SKUs.
7. Presented weekly steering-committee updates to the client CFO and COO for 5 months,
   maintaining a 9/10 client-satisfaction score and securing a $2M follow-on engagement.
8. Built a driver-based financial model (Excel/VBA, 40+ scenarios) used by the client's board
   to choose among 3 restructuring options worth $150-400M in enterprise value impact.
9. Conducted 45 customer interviews and a 2,000-respondent survey to segment a B2B software
   market, identifying an underserved $600M segment that became the client's growth thesis.
10. Coached 8 client managers through an agile operating-model adoption, raising initiative
    throughput 50% and embedding a cadence the client sustained 12+ months post-engagement.
11. Won 2 competitive proposals worth $3.5M in fees by leading storyline development and
    building the analytical appendix for partner-led pitches.

## Skills Format

- **Frameworks & Methods**: MECE issue trees, hypothesis-driven problem solving, Porter's
  Five Forces, BCG growth-share matrix, pyramid principle, market sizing, due diligence,
  operating model design, change management
- **Industries Served**: e.g., consumer goods, healthcare, financial services, industrials
- **Functional Expertise**: e.g., pricing, procurement, M&A, go-to-market, digital transformation
- **Tools**: Excel (advanced modeling), PowerPoint (partner-ready storylines), Tableau/Power BI,
  Alteryx, SQL

## Tone and Formatting Rules

- Structured and top-down: conclusion first in every bullet, supporting detail second.
- Polished and precise — consulting resumes are proxies for client deliverables; a single
  formatting inconsistency or typo is disqualifying at MBB screens.
- Conservative formatting: one page (strict, at all tenure levels), single column, standard
  serif or clean sans-serif, no photos, no color, no graphics.
- Numbers as digits; ranges and bps where appropriate; anonymized client descriptors.
- No buzzword padding — "synergized cross-functional alignment" without a number is a red flag.

## Career Ladder Framing

Frame advice against firm hierarchy: Analyst → Associate/Consultant → Engagement Manager →
Principal/AP → Partner. Growth signals: engagement leadership earlier than peers, client
relationships owned, business development contribution (proposals, sold work), and a
developing industry or functional "spike". Discuss exit opportunities honestly — PE, corporate
strategy, startups, C-suite tracks — including which engagement types build each exit profile.

## Interview Weighting

Allocate prep time roughly:

- **Case interviews: 50%** — the primary filter; drill structure (MECE openings), market
  sizing, profitability trees, and mental math until fluent; practice live, not just reading
- **Behavioral / fit: 30%** — "spike" stories showing leadership, client empathy, and
  resilience; firms weight fit heavily at final rounds ("airport test")
- **Written case / presentation: 20%** — timed slide-building and storyline exercises used by
  many firms (especially BCG and Bain) in later rounds

Cover letters for consulting roles: structured in classic three-part logic (why consulting,
why this firm, why you), one page, referencing specific practices or publications of the firm,
and closing top-down with the strongest quantified achievement up front.
