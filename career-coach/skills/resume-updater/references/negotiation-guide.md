# Offer Negotiation Guide

Practical tactics for negotiating job offers. Used by `/compare-offers` to ground
negotiation talking points. Every tactic here assumes the candidate negotiates in
good faith — never invent competing offers or fabricate numbers.

## Core Principles

1. **Never negotiate before you have to.** Deflect salary questions until an offer
   exists: "I'd like to focus on fit first — I'm confident we can align on
   compensation if we both want to move forward."
2. **Everything is negotiable once, at offer time.** Base, equity, sign-on, start
   date, level, review timing. After you sign, leverage drops to near zero.
3. **Negotiate the package, not the salary.** A weak base with a strong sign-on and
   early review can beat a higher base. Compare 4-year totals, not year-1 base.
4. **Enthusiasm plus a number beats an ultimatum.** Every counter should open with
   genuine excitement and close with a specific, benchmarked ask.
5. **The person you negotiate with is rarely the person who decides.** Give the
   recruiter ammunition to advocate for you: data, competing context, and a clear ask.

## Anchoring

- Whoever states the first number sets the anchor. If asked for expectations first,
  give a researched **range whose bottom is your actual target** (e.g., target $185k →
  say "$185k–$210k based on the data I'm seeing for this level").
- Anchor with market data, not personal need: "Levels.fyi and Glassdoor put L5 at this
  company at $190k–$220k base" lands; "I have a mortgage" does not.
- If their offer arrives below your range, re-anchor immediately against the benchmark,
  not against their number: discuss the market midpoint, not "your offer plus 5%".
- Never anchor with your current salary. If asked, redirect: "I'm targeting roles in
  the $X–$Y range, which matches the market for this scope."

## BATNA Framing

Your BATNA (Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement) is what you'll do if this
offer falls through — another offer, your current job, or continued searching.

- **Know it before the first call.** Write down: what happens on day 1 if this offer
  disappears? That number/state is your true floor.
- **Reveal that it exists; keep details vague until useful.** "I'm in late stages with
  two other companies" is leverage. Naming a company invites them to argue relative
  prestige instead of raising the number.
- **A real competing offer is your strongest card.** Share the total-comp figure (not a
  screenshot) once it clears their offer: "Company B is at $X total; you're my first
  choice — can you close the gap?"
- **No BATNA? Don't fake one.** Negotiate on data and enthusiasm instead. A bluffed
  offer that gets called ("we understand — good luck at Company B") ends the negotiation.
- Your current job is a BATNA too: "staying put" makes any move need a meaningful premium.

## Component-by-Component Counter Tactics

| Component | Flexibility | Tactic |
|-----------|-------------|--------|
| **Base salary** | Medium — usually banded per level | Ask for the top of band; if capped, ask what level the next band starts at and whether the role can be re-leveled |
| **Equity** | High at startups, medium at public cos | Often the most movable number; ask for the refresh policy and vesting schedule in the same breath |
| **Sign-on bonus** | High | Easiest "yes" in the package — one-time cost to them; use it to bridge a base gap or replace unvested equity you're walking away from |
| **Annual bonus %** | Low — usually company policy | Don't burn goodwill here; confirm the target % and whether it's prorated year 1 |
| **Level/title** | Medium | Re-leveling moves every band at once — worth more than any single component; ask what evidence would support the higher level |
| **Early review** | High | "Can we put a 6-month compensation review in writing?" — cheap for them, compounding for you |
| **Remote/location** | Varies | If they apply a location adjustment, ask for the exact formula and whether the sign-on can offset year-1 difference |
| **Start date / PTO** | High | Useful final-round asks when money is exhausted |

Sequence: settle level first, then base, then equity, then use sign-on to bridge
whatever gap remains. One consolidated counter beats four serial ones — serial asks
read as moving goalposts.

## Scripts and Phrasing

- **Receiving the offer:** "Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about this. Let me review
  the details and get back to you by [day]." *(Never accept or counter on the call.)*
- **The consolidated counter:** "I want to sign. Based on [Levels.fyi / Glassdoor]
  data for this level and my competing process, I was expecting closer to $X base.
  If you can get to $X and add a $Y sign-on, I'll accept this week."
- **When they say the base is capped:** "Understood. If the base band is fixed, could
  we look at equity or a sign-on to bring year-one total comp to $Z?"
- **Leveraging a competing offer honestly:** "Company B has offered $X total. You're my
  first choice for [specific reason] — if you can get within range of that, this is done."
- **When they push for your current comp:** "I'd rather anchor on the value of this
  role: the market for this scope is $X–$Y, and that's the range I'm targeting."
- **Holding without a counter-number:** "I appreciate you working on this. I need until
  [date] to finish my other process so I can make a final decision fairly."
- **Accepting:** "That works — send the updated letter and I'll sign it today."
  *(Reward the concession with speed; that's why they made it.)*

## Get It in Writing

- **Verbal offers are intentions, not offers.** Do not resign, decline other offers,
  or stop interviewing until the signed written offer is in hand.
- Anything negotiated must appear in the offer letter or an official email from the
  recruiter: base, level, equity grant size AND vesting schedule, sign-on (with any
  clawback terms), bonus target, review commitments, remote-work terms, start date.
- "We'll fix it after you join" is a no. Promises of a fast promotion or an early
  review are worthless unless written. Reply summarizing any phone agreement:
  "Confirming what we discussed: $X base, $Y sign-on, L5, 6-month review — please
  send the updated letter."
- For equity: get the grant in **number of shares/units and the vesting schedule**,
  not just a dollar value, plus the strike price and latest 409A/preferred price for
  options. A dollar value at an arbitrary internal valuation is not comparable data.

## Common Mistakes

- Countering the same day the offer arrives (signals no competing process).
- Negotiating serially instead of one consolidated ask.
- Accepting a deadline as fixed — exploding offers usually extend if you ask.
- Treating the recruiter as an adversary; they are your proxy to the comp committee.
- Winning the negotiation and souring the relationship — the person across the table
  is often your future manager. Firm on numbers, warm in tone, fast once they meet you.
